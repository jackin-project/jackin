**Research state:** Current

## Summary

The refreshed literature supports experiments at specific boundaries; it does not establish a universal compressor or prove that today's products preserve coding quality. Controlled agent studies, format benchmarks, fixed-trajectory accounting and production observations answer different questions.

Three findings change the earlier map. **AgentDiet** supplies controlled coding-agent reduction results that include auxiliary-model expense and cache effects. **TurnCost** explains how cache assumptions and trajectory selection change economic conclusions, but does not execute compressed counterfactual tasks. **CompactionBench/APEC** adds promising current vendor evidence whose public record was limited to an abstract in this audit. These cannot be collapsed into one savings percentage.

Two historical citations were wrong: arXiv **2504.11154** is an Earth-observation paper, and **2408.11348** is a particle-filtering paper. They did not support LLMRouterBench or OrcaRouter. Correct sources and a further paper-title/version change are recorded below.

## Question, cutoff and evidence method

Which mechanisms transfer to Jackin's actual control surface while retaining the selected model, effort, permissions and accepted-work standard?

The source snapshot is **2026-10-24 01:15 UTC**. Paper versions, release pins and artifact revisions are explicit; live pages were retrieved later and their timestamps/hashes are retained. A paper's publication date is not a product release date. The coverage ledger maps every earlier heading and citation to a current disposition.

## Long-running agents: three different kinds of evidence

### AgentDiet: controlled reduction with an auxiliary model

[AgentDiet v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23586v2), revised March 15, 2026, integrates a delayed sliding-window reducer into **Trae Agent**. Its main models are Claude 4 Sonnet and Gemini 2.5 Pro; a separate GPT-5 mini reduces selected older steps. Hyperparameters were tuned on 100 SWE-bench Verified instances, then evaluated on 200 different Verified instances and 300 Multi-SWE-bench Flash tasks.

The authors include cached-input effects and reflection expense. They report **39.9–59.7% less accumulated input** and **21.1–35.9% lower total cost including reflection**. Quality point estimates vary:

| Evaluation cell | Original pass rate | AgentDiet pass rate |
|---|---:|---:|
| Verified, Claude 4 Sonnet | 64.5% | 66.5% |
| Verified, Gemini 2.5 Pro | 50.5% | 52.0% |
| Multi-SWE-bench Flash, Claude 4 Sonnet | 40.0% | 39.0% |
| Multi-SWE-bench Flash, Gemini 2.5 Pro | 21.7% | 22.7% |

This is more relevant than a payload-only claim, but “comparable” point estimates do not certify Jackin's noninferiority bound. It preserves the main model while adding another model/service, another cost and a history-rewrite boundary. The [Figshare artifact v2](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.30073654.v2) metadata was retrieved; its 46.8 MB archive was not downloaded or executed.

### TurnCost: a useful accounting challenge, not a quality trial

[TurnCost v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15093v1), October 10, 2026, re-scores **600 existing trajectories**: the first 150 records from each of four OpenHands/SWE-agent × Qwen3.5-122B/Minimax-M2.5 shards. It uses `o200k_base` and simulated append-only prefix reuse, not provider receipts.

Its median observation sizes of 118–286 tokens and approximately 7.1× flat-price inflation describe that sample and near-ideal cache model. The normalized cached-read price is 0.1 of ordinary input; actual write premiums, TTL/block rounding and backend eviction are not modeled. Corpus filtering excludes iteration-limit runs, empty patches and other cases, potentially removing important expensive failures.

The paper explicitly does not measure compressed-counterfactual quality. Its turn-count/cost association is partly mechanical and does not prove removable turns. Its cost parity between truncation and learned delegation cannot establish equivalent acceptance. The advertised [Zenodo artifact](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22216661) was not retrieved in this audit. Reuse the accounting questions and sensitivity analysis, then replay accepted tasks under current prices.

### CompactionBench/APEC: current vendor evidence with an access limit

The [October 2026 Microsoft Research abstract](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/benchmarking-long-horizon-agent-context-compaction-with-compactionbench/) describes **13.5 million Copilot sessions** as an observational analysis: 7.8% of sessions involving compaction account for 44.2% of tokens. That is a concentration finding, not the treatment's causal saving.

A separate benchmark evaluates APEC, an asynchronous progressive-compaction method intended to preserve cache reuse, with vendor-reported reductions **up to 25.4% in cost and 9.6% in latency**, alongside improved quality. The public abstract did not expose the full paper, code, configurations or raw paired outcomes in this refresh. Therefore the mechanism is a current design lead, not an executed or independently qualified Jackin optimizer. Do not assign the observational cohort size to the controlled comparison.

### Consequence for experiment design

These studies jointly reject two shortcuts: treating every byte removed as full-price input saved, and treating observation size as the only cause of agent cost. Preserve complete request/response accounting, include failed and abandoned attempts, and distinguish fewer tokens per turn from fewer turns per accepted task. Current native compaction, cache reads/writes, schemas and truncation belong in the baseline.

They also reject a blanket conclusion that reduction cannot help. AgentDiet actually changes the agent's behavior and measures outcomes; TurnCost holds traces fixed. Their apparent tension is largely a difference in the question being measured.

## Style and formats: token reductions can change the task

### CAVEWOMAN is not a current Caveman-product benchmark

The [CAVEWOMAN paper v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.24083v1) studies eight core models, five benchmark datasets and 11,465 examples. It separates input POS filtering from output-style prompts. Output compression benefits 17 of 20 API evaluation cells at the reported first level, but intensity changes both style prompts and token budgets. Greedy decoding, answer extraction, hidden reasoning and reference-NLI agreement constrain the interpretation.

The [expanded repository at `28875b39`](https://github.com/danielle34/cavewoman/tree/28875b39a6c957a0ae96950538cdacfcd0eff0b3) adds a ninth model and extensions to eight datasets, with a judge-corrected **18/20** statement. These denominators must not be merged. The repository contains sample generations and headline tables, not the claimed full generation corpus for independent recomputation.

Neither version evaluates the pinned modern Caveman engine/proxy or long coding sessions. It supports testing style as a model-sensitive treatment; it does not authorize removal of caveats, evidence, tests or architecture work. See [the style chapter](/research/context/techniques/04-style-and-language-compression/) for native concise baselines and executed proxy-count fixtures.

### Notation Matters supplies independent counterevidence

[Notation Matters v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29676v2), June 17, 2026, evaluates five open model configurations across BFCL4, MCPToolBench++, MCPUniverse and StableToolBench. It reports maximum TRON token reduction of 27% with accuracy drops up to 14 percentage points, and TOON reduction of 18% with drops up to nine points. These maxima are study-specific and need not occur in the same evaluation cell.

The input-only treatment changes both schemas and responses; full treatment also changes emitted calls. Models, parser behavior, unavailable API/browser tasks and baseline parsing failures affect the result. It does not prove that every JSON minification or table encoding fails, nor that frontier models behave identically.

The [code at `b31385bb`](https://github.com/lkutschka/notation-matters/tree/b31385bb66ffdfd0b9da7eeaef03870a74aeafb1) is versioned; its root/shared-format MIT license does not replace licenses in vendored benchmarks. Jackin should test typed, reversible representation at an owned boundary and then measure comprehension and accepted work.

## Learned prompt compression and integrity

The LLMLingua family must be described by mechanism. Earlier causal-model perplexity pruning, question-aware LongLLMLingua and [LLMLingua-2's bidirectional token classifier](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12968v2) are not interchangeable. Compression-model weights, latency, additional tokenization and task distribution matter. The [comparative trade-off study, 2505.00019v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.00019v1), evaluates multiple methods and datasets and supports task-sensitive evaluation rather than a global reduction target. The [frontier audit](/research/context/techniques/14-frontier-ideas/) separately covers trained gist/soft-context techniques and their model-interface requirements.

A smaller prompt can lower quality, and a cached larger prompt can sometimes be cheaper. That is a conditional economic possibility, not a universal prohibition on learned reduction. Stable transformed segments, changed suffixes, native cache eligibility and future reuse determine the result. Current provider-specific equations replace the earlier fixed 5.5× break-even assertion.

[CompressionAttack v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.22963v2) studies adversarial manipulation of hard and soft compression through HardCom and SoftCom. Its QA/preference tests and Cline/Ollama case studies show that a compressor can change the downstream interpretation of untrusted content. Reported attack success under those conditions is not a production incidence estimate.

For Jackin, the relevant test is whether filtering preserves the boundary between instructions and untrusted results, including negations, permission failures, partial scans, executable snippets and exact recovery identifiers. Preserve exit status and stderr independently of a summary. A lower token count does not compensate for converting incomplete evidence into an apparently successful result.

## KV cache and bounded-corpus reuse

| Study | Mechanism at the serving boundary | Hosted-agent implication |
|---|---|---|
| [H2O v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14048v3) | Evict KV positions while retaining recent and high-attention tokens. | Requires inference-state control; its throughput/memory figures are not hosted bill reductions. |
| [SnapKV v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14469v2) | Select clustered prompt KV positions per attention head from an observation window. | Changes internal attention state, not the bytes sent to a hosted API. Quality must be measured for the serving model and cache budget. |
| [PyramidKV v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02069v4) | Allocate different KV budgets by transformer layer. | Layer-dependent memory savings do not imply fewer billed input tokens or accepted coding tasks. |
| [KVQuant v6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.18079v6) | Quantize KV activations with outlier handling and custom kernels. | Relevant to self-hosted memory/capacity; precision and hardware kernels are additional variables. |
| [CAG v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15605v2) | Preload a constrained knowledge corpus and reuse inference state. | A hosted analogue is an eligible stable prefix, with paid writes/reads, capacity, freshness and access controls. It is not portable KV import or free unlimited repository memory. |

The old serving/hosted distinction survives, but “stable prefix” needs the actual provider contract. The current [infrastructure chapter](/research/context/techniques/13-infrastructure-level/) covers deployment ownership, and [fleet caching](/research/context/techniques/22-fleet-and-multitenant-cache/) covers tenancy and synchronization costs. A repository index, an on-disk source cache, a model's KV cache and an injected conversation summary are separate state.

## Routing: correct sources and a separate experiment

| Study | What the primary evidence supports | Qualification limit |
|---|---|---|
| [RouteLLM v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18665v4) | Preference-trained routing between models can improve selected cost/quality trade-offs. | Query-level results do not establish multi-turn cache continuity, escalation cost or fixed-model compression gains. |
| [LLMRouterBench, ACL Findings 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1881/) | Over 400K instances, 21 datasets, 33 models and ten router baselines. Model complementarity exists, yet several recent/commercial methods do not reliably beat a simple baseline under unified evaluation. | A router's sophistication is not evidence of benefit. Evaluation coverage, task mix and model availability still differ from Jackin's sessions. |
| [OrcaRouter v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30736v1) | Hybrid offline/online contextual-bandit routing; offline full-information evaluation initializes per-model estimators, then optional online updates use selected-arm feedback. | The May 2026 RouterArena result is a particular snapshot. Training/evaluation expense, selection bias, latency and coding-task/cache behavior need separate accounting. |
| [TRACE-ROUTER v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22465v1) → [ORACLE v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22465v4) | The revised work binds model/verifier by task, adds task-appropriate delayed verification and DISC admission based on expected KV use. It studies concurrent agent workloads and backend contention. | Current v4 results are not the July TRACE results. Self-hosted KV capacity, verifier accuracy and alternate backend dispatch change the treatment. The current abstract/introduction were inspected; code and full paired result reproduction were not. |

Task-level model pinning can avoid one source of cache churn, but it still chooses a different model. The user's main model and effort remain fixed in the central comparison. Any routing arm must disclose the model pool, training/calibration costs, verifier calls, in-flight concurrency, fallback behavior and all failed attempts; it must not silently replace hard work with a cheaper model or omit review/testing.

## Market delta and independent evidence

The latest market is already broader than the old “white space” list: products implement tool filtering, cache warming, request rewriting, exact-original stores, context editing and receipt attribution. The [pinned market scan](/research/context/techniques/03-prior-art-and-market-scan/) distinguishes permissive libraries from GPL, dual-use, ELv2 and preview-licensed applications. License eligibility and benchmark quality are separate questions.

There is also independent evidence beyond vendor READMEs. The [benchmark methodology audit](/research/context/tools/07-evidence-and-claims/) reopens the relevant JetBrains, Quesma, CodePointer and other studies, preserving their versions, tasks and exclusions. This refresh does not substitute publication count for replication quality. A single repeated task, a payload microbenchmark and a multi-repository task set have different coverage.

Several current public numbers require special care:

- CAVEWOMAN's paper and expanded repository have different model/cell counts.
- TOON's current retrieval benchmark includes structural metadata tests and a separate CSV subset.
- Paritok's raw-model trial shows an observed acceptance drop; a nonsignificant p-value does not prove noninferiority.
- CodeDB's combined result is dominated by two feature tasks in a twelve-task trial.
- Boost's introduction and benchmark page report different task/cost denominators; the latter's 81/89 number is reporting coverage, not successful tasks.

The complete comparisons and primary links are in [03's denominator table](/research/context/techniques/03-prior-art-and-market-scan/#published-numbers-and-their-denominators). These are reasons to design a better local comparison, not reasons to ignore the mechanisms.

## Implications for jackin❯

Prioritize experiments that keep the primary model/effort and native harness stable: bounded deterministic reduction of known output, typed representation, exact versioned retrieval, stable-prefix composition and carefully scoped semantic navigation. Reuse a narrow library when it supplies a needed primitive; avoid adopting an entire workflow because one inner transform is useful.

Automatic operation is the target after qualification. For an already-authorized captured result, unsupported shapes, broken storage or failed fidelity checks preserve the original result and record the cause. Authorization or path-integrity failures stop access; they do not permit raw fallback from an unverified source. A transform should not require the user to manage every ordinary command, nor silently hide that the optimized path was unavailable.

Promotion uses the [current acceptance protocol](/research/context/techniques/16-validation-harness/): zero new critical failures; a one-sided confidence bound on acceptance difference; all-attempt cost per accepted result; and task latency, resource use and quota evidence where observable. A small pilot finds defects but cannot establish a narrow population-wide quality guarantee. Offline counts and source tests qualify their local contracts only.

The remaining uncertainties are concrete: actual native interception across hosts, recovery before exact edits, cold/warm index economics, provider cache-write amplification, effects on delayed task information, and subscription quota conversion. The research supports measuring those quantities; it does not establish a broad current product winner or authorize production activation.
