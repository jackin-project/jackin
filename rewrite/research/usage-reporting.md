Status: finalized specification, 2026-11-06. Repository baseline: jackin `feature/openshell-native` at `845d128da0b839befab4dc69f24c0e748fc25069` (2026-11-06), incorporating usage research commit `4e58311c18d04e41d930f8eddb010fa812e6a36d` and authentication research commit `845d128da0b839befab4dc69f24c0e748fc25069`. The usage crates, account core, CLI usage command, console usage wiring (`P9.3` through `P9.5`), design, and plan were inspected at this baseline. [DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md) remains authoritative; the implementation status below reflects this baseline. Sanitized results from explicitly authorized, read-only live checks are recorded below. Credential values and raw responses are not included. A credential was accidentally exposed in an earlier research tool result; the incident is described below without reproducing its value. Provider documentation can change; recheck it before implementing.

## Scope and evidence rules

This specification covers how Jackin obtains and reports provider usage, limits, balances, and costs. The companion [agent-provider proposal](agent-provider.md) covers account records and agent bindings; shared identity and credential boundaries are described here only as they affect usage collection. Both proposals remain subordinate to DESIGN.md.

A provider name, a crate, a fixture, or a successful request for one account does not establish support for every product, account, region, or authentication method. The global subscription update below narrows the scope and records the latest request evidence. This report distinguishes **verified** repository behavior and official documentation, **inference** from those facts, **proposal**, and **unknown**. “Undocumented” means this research did not find a provider-supported contract for the exact endpoint, not that access is forbidden by definition. A vendor's UI is not a machine API. Each future support claim names the exact product, credential, metric, endpoint, version, and evidence date.

Current Jackin distinguishes the AI service provider from an OpenShell provider credential object. Accounts have a provider and credential source. Codex is the only supported agent. Usage collectors operate on the host and read credentials from Jackin accounts; they do not use the OpenShell gateway and do not refresh login tokens. D10, D25, and D26 in [DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md) define these boundaries. The baseline contains collectors for Claude, Codex, Cursor, Gemini, Grok, Kimi, MiniMax, OpenCode, OpenRouter, and Z.AI; Amp, Antigravity, Hermes, and Muse report unsupported. Presence of a collector or fixture does not establish that its provider contract is reliable or its behavior matches a provider subscription.

## Authorized account verification on 2026-11-04

The operator authorized read-only checks of the locally signed-in subscriptions. The operator supplied current displays for Claude, Codex, Grok, Antigravity, Kimi, Z.AI, MiniMax Code, MiMo, Muse and OpenCode. - The OpenCode console displayed five-hour, weekly and monthly usage windows, plan renewal and a separate extra-usage balance. Its listed model catalog is not proof of separate per-model quota pools.

## Existing architecture and corrections

The [facade model](../../crates/usage/jackin-usage/src/model.rs) is schema 1: each named account has one provider, freshness, and either windows or an unavailable reason. Its `WindowKind` also represents credits and spending. A `Percent` is restricted to 0–100, and the facade clamps reported percentages into that range. The Codex collector also clamps individual-limit remaining amounts to zero with `Money::clamped_at_zero`. Therefore the current facade cannot preserve all provider-reported overage or negative remaining amounts. It cannot represent both successful observations and a failure from another endpoint, distinguish stale data from the cause of refresh failure, or describe scope and source precisely. An empty window list must not mean zero usage. Preserving provider-reported overage without clamping is a proposed future behavior, not current behavior.

The [host](../../crates/usage/jackin-usage/src/host.rs) runs up to four accounts concurrently, caches for 120 seconds, or 300 seconds for Claude, honors a retry hold after rate limiting, and can return stale data on transient failure. [Cache](../../crates/usage/jackin-usage/src/cache.rs) writes private mode 0600 under a lock and merges edits. Its fingerprint includes provider and credential source location; for a 1Password source it also includes the optional account selector. It does not include the identity of the credential material at that source. Replacing a credential at the same path, environment name, or 1Password reference and account can show the old account's cached data. Concurrent same-account completions can overwrite a newer result. Fix identity and ordering before expanding support. Do not hash low-entropy secrets as a durable identity.

[Credential resolution](../../crates/usage/jackin-usage/src/credential.rs) reads environment, `op read` (including the configured optional account selector), a file, or Codex login. It infers login JSON by a leading `{` and does not refresh. [Dispatch](../../crates/usage/jackin-usage/src/dispatch.rs) maps both HTTP 401 and 403 to expired login, including API-key and permission failures. [OpenRouter](../../crates/usage/jackin-usage-provider-openrouter/src/lib.rs) tolerates credits 403 without a partial-result reason. Inspect the Grok collector's signed-cent magnitude and missing on-demand defaults against the real contract before treating either as a valid balance. Inspect OpenCode's fixed 30-day monthly duration against actual calendar and response semantics. These are correctness investigations, not claims that the upstream contract has been proven.

The [Grok collector](../../crates/usage/jackin-usage-provider-grok/src/lib.rs) currently applies an absolute value to signed cent amounts and treats a missing or non-boolean `on_demand_enabled` as enabled. Its fixtures encode those choices and use `"PRODUCT_GROK_BUILD"` in `productUsage[]`, whereas the live-observed 2026-11-04 response returned `"product": "GrokBuild"` and the collector ignores `productUsage[]` altogether. In addition, `login_key` in [`crates/usage/jackin-usage-provider-grok/src/lib.rs`](../../crates/usage/jackin-usage-provider-grok/src/lib.rs) selects the first map entry whose key starts with `"https://auth.x.ai::"` (falling back to `"/sign-in"`), ignores `GROK_AUTH`, `GROK_AUTH_PATH`, and `GROK_HOME`, and evaluates `expires_at` with `is_some_and(|at| at <= now)`, which fails open (`false`) when `expires_at` is absent, numeric, or unparseable. Its `period_window` helper also computes window duration solely by subtracting `start` from `end` while ignoring `config.currentPeriod.type` (such as `"USAGE_PERIOD_TYPE_WEEKLY"`). Until official documentation or a matching live response establishes the semantics, the proposed model must not turn a negative amount into a positive balance, infer enabled status from a missing flag, or fail open on unverified expiry; retain the signed observation without labeling its meaning, or report that result unavailable. Test negative amounts, missing and malformed flags, explicit true and false, `"GrokBuild"` product rows, `currentPeriod.type` classification, and fail-closed `expires_at` parsing through the public interface. These are proposed corrections to unsupported assumptions, not claims about how Grok's backend defines those fields.

**Official Grok Build client types, source-only.** The pinned [billing handler](https://github.com/xai-org/grok-build/blob/b189869b7755d2b482969acf6c92da3ecfeffd36/crates/codegen/xai-grok-shell/src/extensions/billing.rs) describes `Cent.val` as signed USD cents and defaults an omitted `val` to zero when a `Cent` object is present. This does not make a missing optional money object, or an explicit null `val`, zero. The client describes `prepaid_balance` as remaining purchased credit, positive for topped-up credits, and says it is absent in the legacy billing shape; negative-value meaning remains unspecified. Its optional `on_demand_enabled` is populated from client-side `RemoteSettings` after the billing response is parsed; the source says explicit false hides on-demand controls, while missing remains unknown. Do not assume that client-enriched flag is present in the raw proxy response. The handler also logs a billing snapshot, so invoking it as a verification shortcut would conflict with this research's no-response-logging boundary. These are source-defined client behaviors, not a published upstream wire guarantee or live proof for every plan.

The [OpenCode collector](../../crates/usage/jackin-usage-provider-opencode/src/lib.rs) assigns 30 days to every monthly bucket. Its [fixture](../../crates/usage/jackin-usage/tests/usage/fixtures/opencode/usage.json) supplies a single reset timestamp, and the [fixture tests](../../crates/usage/jackin-usage/tests/usage/providers/opencode.rs) check the monthly kind and reset timestamp rather than establishing its duration. The successful local-service read established a monthly percentage, not a fixed-length period. Preserve the provider's monthly label and reported reset; leave duration unknown until its contract supports a value.

The current resolver does not automatically read every installed agent's credential store. In [`crates/usage/jackin-usage/src/credential.rs`](../../crates/usage/jackin-usage/src/credential.rs), file-backed credentials delegate to [`jackin_core::read_credential_file`](../../crates/jackin-core/src/fs.rs), which canonicalizes operator symlinks first and then verifies a regular file descriptor capped at 1 MiB under D26. When an operator selects a directory in the TUI account form, [`Provider::login_file`](../../crates/jackin-core/src/provider.rs) maps `Amp` to `secrets.json`, `Claude` to `.credentials.json`, `Codex`, `Cursor`, `Grok`, `Muse`, and `Opencode` to `auth.json`, `Gemini` to `oauth_creds.json`, `Kimi` to `credentials/kimi-code.json`, and returns `None` for `Antigravity`, `Hermes`, `Minimax`, `Openrouter`, and `Zai`. [Claude's parser](../../crates/usage/jackin-usage-provider-claude/src/lib.rs) accepts a raw token or login JSON with `claudeAiOauth.accessToken` or `claudeAiOauth.access_token`, but does not resolve the observed Keychain login itself. [Codex](../../crates/usage/jackin-usage-provider-codex/src/lib.rs) reads `tokens.access_token` from login JSON and receives the ChatGPT account context separately; the special source resolves `<codex-home>/auth.json`. [OpenCode Go's parser](../../crates/usage/jackin-usage-provider-opencode/src/lib.rs) accepts a raw token or an `opencode-go` entry in login JSON, but cannot directly consume the observed SQLite credential table. [Gemini's parser](../../crates/usage/jackin-usage-provider-gemini/src/lib.rs) accepts a token or JSON `access_token` and optional `expiry_date`, not the observed Antigravity Go-keyring record; its Code Assist requests are also different from the Antigravity quota-summary request. Z.AI, MiniMax, and OpenRouter dispatch require the resolver's token variant rather than a login variant; this does not generally validate the token format or accept an arbitrary agent database record. Furthermore, [`crates/jackin-tui/src/project.rs`](../../crates/jackin-tui/src/project.rs) maps [`jackin_core::Provider`](../../crates/jackin-core/src/provider.rs) exhaustively 1-to-1 onto `jackin_console::domain::agent::Provider` pinned in `tailrocks/terminal-components-baseline`, so adding a new provider variant such as `Provider::Mimo` requires a coordinated baseline update. A discovered login does not itself establish a usable Jackin credential source. Any future adapter must resolve the selected product-specific source securely without printing secrets or assuming that a credential for one product covers another.

The [CLI](../../crates/jackin/src/commands/usage.rs) reports every configured account, uses cached mode, and prints text or schema 1 JSON. Before text output, window labels and amount units pass through terminal-text sanitization. It exits successfully for per-account unavailability, and a successful empty window list prints no account line. [P6.3](../PLAN.md) marks the port complete, not all vendor integrations validated. Recorded live provider runs in repository evidence are [Codex P6.3](../evidence/P6.3-usage-codex.md) and [Console P9.5](../evidence/P9.5-console.md), where one ChatGPT account returned a weekly window and credits without token refresh. Fixture data do not establish vendor support. The console uses the external `jackin-console` application through [`crates/jackin-tui/src/console.rs`](../../crates/jackin-tui/src/console.rs), [`crates/jackin-tui/src/usage.rs`](../../crates/jackin-tui/src/usage.rs), and [`crates/jackin-tui/src/readings.rs`](../../crates/jackin-tui/src/readings.rs), which are wired in `P9.3` through `P9.5` and share the host usage cache directory (`WindowKind::FiveHour -> WindowCategory::Session`, `Weekly | Monthly -> WindowCategory::LongRange`, `Credits | Spend | Other -> WindowCategory::Other`, with `Refresh::Force` on account refresh and validation). [D27](../DESIGN.md) assigns screens, wording, and visual approval to `terminal-components-baseline`. No new jackin-local renderer is appropriate.

## Provider contracts and unknowns

Each collector path in the status table is evidence of what code attempts, not authorization or a stable provider contract. Unless explicitly documented below, vendor polling limits, endpoint-specific quotas, token refresh ownership, and regional availability remain unknown. Implementation must establish these or use conservative bounded polling; a generic inference rate limit is not a quota endpoint rate limit.

### Anthropic / Claude

Verified in code: a token or Claude login OAuth access token calls `GET https://api.anthropic.com/api/oauth/usage`; API keys are rejected. It parses subscription windows and extra usage or spending where present. Fixtures cover legacy and newer shapes. No official public contract for this precise OAuth endpoint was established. Unknown: supported OAuth grants, refresh and account eligibility for third-party polling. Never refresh a shared Claude login without an approved documented ownership protocol.

**Claude profile-specific Keychain selection, installed-source evidence.** In the inspected Claude Code 2.1.318 binary, the service name is `Claude Code`, an OAuth environment suffix where applicable, `-credentials`, and an optional profile suffix. A defined `CLAUDE_SECURESTORAGE_CONFIG_DIR` takes precedence over `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR`, even when empty; an empty secure-storage override suppresses the profile suffix. When the selected override is nonempty, the installed client NFC-normalizes its directory string and appends a hyphen plus the first eight hexadecimal characters of its SHA-256 digest. Explicitly setting a nonempty path equal to the default still takes the suffix path; do not infer equality by comparing normalized filesystem paths. If the secure override is absent, a nonempty `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR` uses the same suffix mechanism; empty suppresses the suffix. Derive any service reference only in memory, never record the derived suffix, and inspect the selected process environment rather than assuming the default profile. These rules are specific to the inspected version; do not use a separately derived cache identifier as a Keychain service name. The [current CodexBar path resolver](https://github.com/steipete/CodexBar/blob/6f72afa7b378f4b2325afe71421946ebabb821b6/Sources/CodexBarCore/Providers/Claude/ClaudeConfigPaths.swift) is third-party evidence and may have different path handling.

Its source selection and cache are CodexBar-specific, not a recommended automatic fallback between accounts. The login JSON may contain `claudeAiOauth` with `accessToken`, `refreshToken`, `expiresAt`, and scope or subscription metadata; an item containing only `mcpOAuth` is not the subscription usage credential. CodexBar reports that the usage request requires `user:profile`; an inference-only token is insufficient. The verified request above used a scoped access token, but the requirement is third-party source evidence rather than a published contract for this endpoint. [Claude Code settings](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/settings) document `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR` as a home-directory override; verify the selected profile's actual credential location and identity rather than borrowing the default profile's Keychain item. Read only the selected access token in memory and do not refresh or copy the credential during research.

Anthropic's [usage guide](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11647753-how-do-usage-and-length-limits-work) says the session allowance ordinarily resets five hours after the first message, weekly limits reset at an account-assigned day and time, and usage across Claude web, Code, and Desktop shares limits. Anthropic's [Fable plan guide](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/15424964-claude-fable-models-on-your-plan) says that, for plans where Fable is included, its model-specific weekly limit draws from the regular weekly allowance. Model-scoped and all-model limits can therefore overlap and must not be summed as independent allowances. The pinned [CodexBar scoped-window parser](https://github.com/steipete/CodexBar/blob/897bff31731bb4b21b105f6e8bf4c32a8a8bf095/Sources/CodexBarCore/Providers/Claude/ClaudeWeb/ClaudeWebExtraRateWindowParser.swift) interprets `limits[]` entries using `kind`, `group`, numeric `percent`, ISO 8601 `resets_at`, and `scope.model.{id,display_name}`. It selects model-scoped weekly entries and rejects invalid numeric percentages. ## Proposed design

### Alternatives and recommendation

1. Extend `LimitWindow` and keep provider-specific exceptions. Small initial diff, but it preserves false equivalence between credits, expense and rate windows and cannot express partial failure. Reject.
2. Store raw vendor JSON and let CLI and console interpret it. Retains details but duplicates provider logic, leaks sensitive fields, and makes stable output impossible. Reject.
3. Recommend a typed facade with explicit per-account capability and observation records. Provider adapters own parsing, scope and source semantics. CLI and console consume the same report. This keeps the existing facade and layer boundary while replacing the schema rather than adding a compatibility shim.

Proposal: the usage facade needs a stable non-secret account identity and enough product, region, account-type and credential-source context to distinguish collections. The account record and agent or workspace bindings belong to the companion agent-provider proposal rather than a second schema here. Multiple accounts of one provider remain independent. A launch's account selection does not imply the usage credential has the same scope, and usage failure never blocks a launch. Deduplicate only when the vendor provides a reliable shared scope identity; never sum accounts with overlapping organization or project scope by default. This requires an explicit amendment to D25/D26 and account configuration before implementation, not an implicit expansion of D04's Codex-only agent policy.

A report has a generation timestamp, version, account ID, provider product, credential capability, collection time, source classification and exact scope. Each observation is one of: rate or quota window (metric, resource/model, scope, period or explicit boundaries, used and limit or remaining, unit and reset); balance/credits (exact decimal amount, unit/currency and scope); or spending (exact decimal, currency, period and scope). Keep vendor-reported used and remaining independently. Compute a derivative only when units, interval and semantics are compatible and label it derived. Preserve overage, negative balances, unlimited and unknown distinctly. Do not fabricate a reset from a monthly label or infer a total from a percentage. Use integer decimal coefficients and scale or equivalent exact decimal representation for money and credits; validate range and unit. Each capability has `available`, `unsupported`, `not_configured`, `permission_denied`, `authentication_failed`, `expired`, `rate_limited`, `temporarily_unavailable`, or `invalid_response`, plus safe details and optional retry time. Add banked reset inventory as a separate observation, including count, optional individual expiry, covered pools and freshness, as detailed in the global subscription research below. A result can contain observations and per-capability failures simultaneously. Unsupported means evidence for the exact product and method is absent or prohibits implementation, not that the vendor has no possible interface.

### Credentials and trust

Resolve only the selected typed source at request time. Reuse `op` for 1Password and preserve existing private host sources where authorized. Separate API, admin, management and subscription credentials; never upgrade privileges or use an inference key as an admin key implicitly. Config stores references and non-secret metadata, not token values. A source location is not a credential identity. Prefer an explicit account identity or credential generation controlled by the user and invalidate the cache on credential replacement. An explicit user-managed generation alone cannot detect external rotation. Persistent cache reuse is permitted only when an authoritative source supplies a non-secret revision that changes on every credential replacement, or when a fresh authenticated response proves the same stable account identity. Otherwise withhold cached observations before a fresh fetch; an explicit rebind clears old data but does not make undetectable later rotation safe. Do not persist a secret-derived fingerprint. Secret-bearing requests use TLS and provider-defined URLs; the shared client disables redirects and emits safe errors. Successful JSON responses are capped at 1 MiB before parsing. Never log headers, token strings, raw responses, account IDs or provider error bodies without a verified redaction policy. Usage data itself is sensitive; store only the minimum normalized data in a private file and support deletion.

Default to no token refresh, consistent with D25. A future refresh implementation requires an official documented grant, explicit ownership, secure storage and atomic rotation, concurrency control, revocation handling, and evidence that it cannot silently invalidate another client. A gateway-held launch token is not automatically accessible to host collectors. Establish a supported and secure host credential source or report unavailable. Do not extract gateway secrets, scrape browser cookies, borrow undocumented client identities, or send consumer subscription credentials to arbitrary endpoints. The existing undocumented direct requests need an explicit product and security decision before continued default polling; vendor documentation and allowed use must be established independently. Opt-in alone is not proof of authorization.

**Proposed enforcement boundaries.** If the selected token is expired or expires during collection, return an explicit expired or authentication-failed result and require a fresh credential from its owner; do not refresh it or call a helper that may refresh it. A preflight margin alone cannot rule out a refresh race: do not use a refresh-capable local route unless its no-refresh execution path is guaranteed or D25 is explicitly revised to permit the refresh; until then, refresh remains prohibited. Never silently fall back to another account, provider, product, region or credential source. Persist only normalized non-secret observations; keep credentials, raw response bodies and unrelated account/session fields out of caches, logs, crash reports and diagnostic output.

### Collection, cache and failure semantics

Proposal: dispatch each supported provider-product-auth capability through a bounded, cancellable read-only adapter. Each declares supported source, endpoint, data scope, timeout, minimum interval and request budget. Coalesce concurrent refreshes by account identity and credential generation. Use a global concurrency cap and per-provider limits; respect `Retry-After` as either seconds or HTTP date, with a bounded retry deadline and jittered exponential backoff for transient read-only failures. Never retry authentication, permission or invalid-response failures automatically. A cancelled request must not commit partial cache data as fresh. The current shared HTTP client disables redirects and caps successful JSON responses at 1 MiB. The proposed report should preserve valid per-endpoint partial success and continue other accounts after one failure.

Cache normalized observations with source, scope, fetch time, identity generation and expiry, in the existing private atomic locked store. Re-read and compare generation and fetch time under the lock so older concurrent responses cannot overwrite newer observations. Define explicit per-provider TTLs once policy is known; treat current 120/300 seconds as existing behavior, not verified vendor rate limits. On transient failure, show stale observations with original fetch time and the current failure; never present them as fresh. On account removal, source change, authorization failure, or identity mismatch, invalidate or withhold data rather than leak a previous account's results. Cache corruption becomes a visible recoverable error rather than fabricated empty usage. Network traffic occurs only on an explicit report or a documented console refresh schedule, with manual refresh still respecting server backoff.

### CLI and console

Proposal: `jackin usage` shows each configured account and scope, every available measurement with units and reset, source freshness, and explicit unsupported or partial reasons. Add account/provider filters and an explicit refresh option with behavior specified before implementation. `--json` returns a versioned structured report with machine-readable statuses, exact decimal strings, timestamps and no raw secrets. Replace schema 1 directly under the no-compatibility rule. Use exit 0 when every selected account has all requested supported capabilities available, including an empty selection; exit 2 for partial results or unsupported capabilities; exit 1 when selected accounts have no available capability because collection or configuration failed; and exit 130 on cancellation. Unsupported-only selection exits 2. A manual refresh bypasses TTL but honors provider backoff and `Retry-After`; if held, report rate-limited with any permitted stale data. Do not turn a single provider's error into missing rows. Stable ordering is by account ID and measurement identity. An empty set is shown as no observations, never as zero.

The console consumes the same facade and renders the same partial, stale, unsupported, scope, reset and privacy semantics. Extend the baseline's `jackin-console` backend model and approved screens in its own repository when needed, with D27 visual reviews and unchanged approved baselines. It must not create a second usage model or polling loop in `jackin-tui`.

The central blocker is that several existing collectors use endpoints without an established supported contract. ### Desired data and reset semantics

For every subscription account, collect the vendor-reported plan, scope, model or shared pool, each available period, used and remaining amount or percentage, and the precise scheduled reset time. A rolling five-hour window is not a daily limit. A fixed 30-day duration is not necessarily a calendar month. A model can share a pool with other models; do not invent separate balances. Distinguish unavailable, not reported, not applicable, and zero. Preserve source timestamps, absolute UTC instants and unknown timezone; localize only for display.

Represent a banked reset separately from scheduled window resets and spendable credits. A banked reset has an available count, optional individual expiry, eligibility, affected windows or model pools, and observation time. Count-only responses remain count-only. Keep the reported count independent from detail rows. Codex explicitly permits a detail list shorter than the count because the backend may cap it; label the list incomplete, preserve valid supplied expiries, and never invent missing dates. If different requests return incompatible snapshots, preserve their timestamps and mark reconciliation uncertain. Never expose redemption IDs in output or persist them when not needed for display. Reporting is read-only; redeeming, buying, and changing settings are outside this specification.

[OpenAI](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001498-how-resets-work-in-codex) documents banked, automatically applied and purchased resets as different operations. Banked expiry is per offer, and a reset can change the next scheduled window boundary. [Anthropic](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/17007452-what-is-a-limit-reset) documents optional limit resets and expiry where applicable; its weekly schedule remains on its usual day and time. Neither source proves that all plans have banked resets. For other providers, “not found” means research found no verified banked-reset contract, not that a reset cannot exist.

### Required corrections to the earlier proposal

1. Prioritize global subscription usage and scheduled/banked resets. Remove organization API analytics and paid API-key reporting from the initial implementation scope unless separately requested.
2. Add a banked-reset inventory type and CLI/console presentation. Show count and each known expiry, affected pool where provided, freshness, and incomplete-detail state. Do not implement a redeem action as part of reporting.
3. Use the source-verified Codex 0.166.0 `account/rateLimits/read` reset detail as the leading candidate. A supported protocol is stronger evidence than a private HTTP request, but the OpenShell execution and authentication boundary still needs proof.
4. Restore historical per-model data only where it represents the same global subscription and the response states the scope. Do not turn locally inferred model activity into per-model account limits.
5. Keep documented and undocumented paths distinct. Preserve the actual request recipes for investigation, including unofficial routes, without claiming they are supported or live-verified. Resolve the product-policy and security decision before enabling them.
6. Review each global product independently. MiMo used and total credits and validity end matched the selected account's console in a later direct check; exact tier mapping and automated-polling permission remain unresolved. OpenRouter is the operator-selected API-key exception; Antigravity, Gemini, Grok Build and Muse must not inherit another product's quota by name.

Every provider milestone must additionally test 5-hour, daily, weekly and monthly windows only when reported; per-model versus shared-pool scope; explicit missing reset; banked count zero, positive count with expiry, count without detail, expired entries, unknown status, capped detail lists and separately fetched inconsistent snapshots; multiple accounts; cancellation and redaction. Independent test-plan and red/green review rules above apply. Live proof requires operator authorization, a read-only request, and a sanitized result containing field names and types, account/product scope and verification timestamp. Never record response bodies, tokens, cookies, account identifiers, grant IDs, or credential files.

### Verified wire details and interpretation

These details describe the cited code, not a claim that private endpoints are supported. The later live verification record identifies which exact requests were tested. Credentials and responses must stay in memory and be redacted. Do not retry or issue a request whose side effects are unknown.

- **Codex 0.166.0.** Send JSON-RPC `account/rateLimits/read` with `excludeResetCreditDetails` omitted or `false`. The response has `rateLimits`, optional `rateLimitsByLimitId`, and `rateLimitResetCredits.availableCount` with nullable `credits`. Individual rows provide `resetType`, `status`, `grantedAt`, and nullable `expiresAt` in Unix seconds. The list may be capped. Unknown types and statuses do not become available resets. See the [pinned protocol](https://github.com/openai/codex/blob/rust-v0.166.0/codex-rs/app-server-protocol/src/protocol/v2/account.rs#L341-L427). Establish how to invoke this protocol within D01 before using it.

  The pinned protocol also defines nullable `ordinaryUsageAllowed` and per-limit snapshots in `rateLimitsByLimitId`. A snapshot may include nullable `limitId`, `limitName`, `normalModelSlug`, `primary`, `secondary`, `credits`, `individualLimit`, `spendControlReached`, and `rateLimitReachedType`. Each present window has integer `usedPercent`, nullable `windowDurationMins` in minutes, and nullable `resetsAt` in Unix seconds. Credit balances and spend-control amounts are strings; keep spend controls separate from subscription windows. Reset detail rows can also include a sensitive `id` and nullable display `title` and `description`; do not record the ID in evidence. These are [source-defined fields](https://github.com/openai/codex/blob/rust-v0.166.0/codex-rs/app-server-protocol/src/protocol/v2/account.rs), not proof that the account supplies them. Null is unavailable, not zero.
